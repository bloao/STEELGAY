Physical route · Airfreight

Air freight

Time-critical airfreight for samples, emergency specialty grades and very high-value, light consignments. Rarely used for commodity steel because of weight economics.

Best suited to

Samples, emergency specialty steel and very high-value or light consignments.

Typical transit

Often 1–5 days door-to-airport-to-door.

Cost characteristics

Extremely expensive for dense material. General 2026 commercial air benchmarks roughly US$2.50–8 per kg; steel’s density typically means actual weight drives the charge.

Main steel risk

Usually economically irrational for bulk steel — reserve for cases where the cost of not having the material dominates the freight bill.

Step by step

  1. Confirm that the high transport cost is commercially justified; this mode usually makes sense for urgent specialty material or samples rather than commodity steel.
  2. Verify supplier, specification, HS code and origin.
  3. Check quota, sanctions and trade-remedy status.
  4. Book airline or forwarder capacity.
  5. Issue commercial invoice and packing list; carrier issues the air waybill.
  6. Provide declaration data to the customs broker before arrival.
  7. Submit CDS declaration with the quota order number where applicable.
  8. Aircraft arrives and cargo is presented to Customs.
  9. Pay or account for duty and import VAT and respond to any inspection request.
  10. Release to courier or road feeder for final delivery.

Key documents

  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial paperwork.
  • Air waybill. Issued by the carrier for the airfreight movement.
  • CDS import declaration. With quota order number where a claim applies.
  • Origin documents. Rules-of-origin evidence where preferential treatment is claimed.
