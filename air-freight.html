Physical route · Airfreight

Air freight

Time-critical airfreight for samples, emergency specialty grades and very high-value, light consignments. Rarely used for commodity steel because of weight economics.

Pricing equation

Risk-adjusted landed cost per tonne (air)

Every cost line that lands in the buyer’s risk-adjusted per-tonne price, plus the default party who bears each. The Incoterm on the contract shifts which side pays several of these; the steel-measure and duty lines always land on the importer of record.

  1. = Ex-mill or ex-works price £ / tonne seller
  2. + Origin inland freight, mill to airport £ / tonne per Incoterm
  3. + Airline chargeable-weight rate £ / kg per Incoterm
  4. + Fuel & security surcharges £ / kg carrier
  5. + Airline handling & air-waybill fee fixed carrier
  6. + Air cargo insurance per mille of declared value typically buyer
  7. + Customs brokerage & CDS filing per entry buyer
  8. + UK ordinary import duty MFN % buyer
  9. + Probability × 50% steel-measure tariff quota-linked buyer
  10. + Anti-dumping & countervailing duty trade-remedy % buyer
  11. + Import VAT (recoverable via PVA) 20% buyer treasury
  12. + UK inland freight £ / tonne buyer
  13. + Storage / demurrage / detention £ / day buyer
  14. + Financing / working capital rate × days buyer
  15. + Non-recoverable indirect costs misc. buyer
Best suited to

Samples, emergency specialty steel and very high-value or light consignments.

Typical transit

Often 1–5 days door-to-airport-to-door.

Cost characteristics

Extremely expensive for dense material. General 2026 commercial air benchmarks roughly US$2.50–8 per kg; steel’s density typically means actual weight drives the charge.

Main steel risk

Usually economically irrational for bulk steel — reserve for cases where the cost of not having the material dominates the freight bill.

Step by step

  1. Confirm that the high transport cost is commercially justified; this mode usually makes sense for urgent specialty material or samples rather than commodity steel.
  2. Verify supplier, specification, HS code and origin.
  3. Check quota, sanctions and trade-remedy status.
  4. Book airline or forwarder capacity.
  5. Issue commercial invoice and packing list; carrier issues the air waybill.
  6. Provide declaration data to the customs broker before arrival.
  7. Submit CDS declaration with the quota order number where applicable.
  8. Aircraft arrives and cargo is presented to Customs.
  9. Pay or account for duty and import VAT and respond to any inspection request.
  10. Release to courier or road feeder for final delivery.

Along the journey — cost & liability

Every variable to price and every point at which contractual liability transfers, arranged in the order they arise on this route.

  1. Contract & pricing

    Where every cost line and legal responsibility is set.

    • Ex-mill / ex-works price. Price per tonne against the mill specification, before any handling or logistics.
    • Purchase currency & FX. Invoice currency, hedging responsibility and any non-recoverable bank charges.
    • Payment terms. Letter of credit, telegraphic transfer or open account — each has a different treasury and default profile.
    • Incoterm 2020. Sets the exact point at which cost and risk pass from seller to buyer for every subsequent stage.
    • Steel-measure allocation. Contract clause on which party bears an unexpected 50% out-of-quota duty.
    • Anti-dumping / sanctions warranty. Seller warranty on non-Russian origin and third-country-processing compliance.
    • Retention of title. Whether the seller retains title to the material until payment clears.
    • Warranty & quality. Mill certificates, testing regime and remedy for non-conformity.

  2. At origin

    From the mill to the outbound port, terminal or airport.

    • Inland origin freight. Truck, rail or barge cost from the mill to the export point.
    • Export documentation. Commercial invoice, packing list, mill certificate, origin declaration.
    • Export customs formalities. Usually the seller’s obligation; the cost varies by jurisdiction.
    • Origin terminal handling (OTHC). Loading, stuffing and outbound port or terminal charges.
    • Insurance up to load point. Cover for the inland leg to the export gateway.

  3. Main transport

    The airfreight leg — typically 1–5 days including handling either side.

    • Chargeable-weight rate. Priced per kilo on the greater of gross weight and dimensional weight (usually gross for dense steel).
    • Fuel & security surcharges. Standard airline surcharges on top of the base rate.
    • Dangerous-goods handling. Additional cost for any coating or chemistry classed as dangerous by air.
    • Air cargo insurance. Typically a per-mille rate on declared value; may be mandatory under Incoterm.
    • Airline handling & documentation. Terminal handling both ends and air-waybill issue fee.
    • Time-definite delivery premium. Guaranteed slots and priority handling carry a further surcharge.

  4. UK arrival & customs

    Duty, VAT and every trade-policy check applied to the consignment.

    • Destination terminal handling (DTHC). Discharge and outbound handling at the UK port, terminal or airport.
    • Customs brokerage & CDS filing. Broker fee or in-house filing cost for the import declaration.
    • UK ordinary import duty. MFN rate on the applicable commodity code, where any survives preferential treatment.
    • 2026 steel measure. Quota order number claim in CDS DE 8/1; a 50% out-of-quota tariff attaches if the quota has closed.
    • Anti-dumping & countervailing. Separate trade-remedy duties on top of ordinary duty and the steel measure.
    • Sanctions screening. Russian-origin iron and steel and third-country-processed material prohibitions.
    • Import VAT. 20% of customs value plus duty, usually recoverable via postponed VAT accounting.

  5. Post-clearance & delivery

    From UK release to the buyer’s site, plus working capital and audit.

    • UK inland freight. Truck or intermodal from the UK entry point to the buyer’s site.
    • Storage, demurrage & detention. Time-based charges if the container, trailer or berth is not released promptly.
    • Financing / working capital. Cost of tying up cash between payment to the seller and sale or consumption.
    • Quality inspection at receipt. Third-party or in-house quality check on arrival.
    • Record retention. Declaration, quota confirmation, origin evidence and commercial records for the statutory audit period.

Key documents

  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial paperwork.
  • Air waybill. Issued by the carrier for the airfreight movement.
  • CDS import declaration. With quota order number where a claim applies.
  • Origin documents. Rules-of-origin evidence where preferential treatment is claimed.
