Physical route · Rail & intermodal

Rail & intermodal

Long-haul rail and intermodal from Eurasian origins, plus shorter European rail flows. A middle ground on cost and speed but exposed to multiple customs jurisdictions.

Best suited to

Selected Eurasian and intermodal movements and European rail flows.

Typical transit

China–UK commercial services can be in the 23–27-day range; European routes are much shorter.

Cost characteristics

A middle ground between sea and air but highly route-dependent.

Main steel risk

Transits multiple customs and geopolitical jurisdictions; transit-procedure paperwork must be complete end to end.

Step by step

  1. Verify the supplier, country of origin and route.
  2. Determine whether the journey uses Common Transit Convention procedures or other transit regimes.
  3. Classify goods and check UK tariffs, quotas, sanctions and trade remedies before departure.
  4. Book rail or intermodal capacity and the first-mile road collection.
  5. Prepare invoice, packing list, origin evidence and rail consignment documentation.
  6. Open any required transit movement and obtain its movement reference or document.
  7. Cargo travels to the UK rail terminal or Channel crossing.
  8. Complete UK CDS import formalities and close the relevant transit procedure.
  9. Claim the steel quota through CDS if applicable.
  10. Pay or account for duties and VAT.
  11. Release for final UK road or rail delivery.

Key documents

  • Rail consignment / transit document. Transport and, where relevant, transit paperwork.
  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial paperwork.
  • Origin documents. Preferential evidence where applicable.
  • CDS import declaration. Quota order number in DE 8/1 for a quota claim.
