Physical route · Barge & estuary

Inland waterway

Barge and estuary movements for heavy lots between UK seaports and inland or riverine sites. Almost always a domestic post-port leg after an international sea import.

Best suited to

Heavy lots moved domestically from port to a river- or estuary-connected site.

Typical transit

Usually a domestic post-port leg rather than an international UK entry mode.

Cost characteristics

Potentially efficient for very heavy bulk where the infrastructure exists.

Main steel risk

Limited UK network and terminal access; does not replace the border customs procedure that applied on arrival at the seaport.

Step by step

  1. Supplier dispatches by sea.
  2. Cargo arrives at a UK seaport or estuary terminal.
  3. Complete the UK import declaration or place the goods under an appropriate customs procedure or transit arrangement.
  4. Transfer coil, plate, billet or other material from the ocean vessel or terminal into the barge.
  5. Move through the navigable inland or estuarial waterway.
  6. Discharge at the inland terminal or customer site.
  7. If goods remained under customs control, complete the customs procedure before unrestricted use.

Key documents

  • Sea-freight arrival papers. Bill of lading and port paperwork from the sea import leg.
  • Customs procedure declaration. Warehousing or transit paperwork if not entering free circulation at the seaport.
  • Barge / inland transport records. Consignment or booking evidence for the domestic waterway leg.
