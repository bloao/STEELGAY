Customs procedure · Duty & VAT suspension

Customs / bonded warehouse

Store imported steel under HMRC-authorised customs warehousing with duty and import VAT suspended until the goods are released to free circulation — or re-exported without ever paying UK import duty.

Pricing equation

Landed cost with customs warehousing

Every cost line that lands in the buyer’s risk-adjusted per-tonne price, plus the default party who bears each. The Incoterm on the contract shifts which side pays several of these; the steel-measure and duty lines always land on the importer of record.

  1. = Underlying import cost to UK gateway route-dependent as per chosen physical route
  2. + Warehouse entry declaration + receipt fee per consignment buyer
  3. + Storage rate £ / day · pallet, coil or m3 buyer
  4. + Handling and movement in bond per operation buyer
  5. + Insurance while in bond % or flat buyer / warehouse policy
  6. − Working-capital benefit of deferred duty & VAT rate × days buyer
  7. + Warehouse release fee + free-circulation declaration per consignment buyer
  8. + Ordinary import duty at release MFN % buyer
  9. + Probability × 50% steel-measure tariff (at release) quota-linked buyer
  10. + Anti-dumping & countervailing duty trade-remedy % buyer
  11. + Import VAT at release (recoverable via PVA) 20% buyer treasury
  12. + UK inland freight from warehouse £ / tonne buyer
Best suited to

Inventory awaiting sale, further processing, a quota decision or re-export.

Typical transit

Storage can be days to months, ended by release to free circulation, transfer to another procedure or re-export.

Cost characteristics

Storage, handling and administration cost; duty and import VAT cash payment suspended while under the procedure.

Main steel risk

Quota may exhaust while the goods sit in the warehouse — the claim is made on release to free circulation, not on arrival.

Step by step

  1. Arrange the overseas purchase and transport normally.
  2. On UK arrival, place goods under the authorised customs warehousing procedure instead of immediately entering free circulation.
  3. Submit the required warehouse customs declaration.
  4. Move goods to the authorised warehouse and record them in the warehouse inventory system.
  5. Customs Duty and import VAT remain suspended while the procedure is correctly maintained.
  6. Goods can potentially be re-exported without ever entering UK free circulation.
  7. When a UK sale or use is required, check the quota balance again at that time.
  8. Submit the declaration releasing the steel to free circulation.
  9. Claim the quota order number if quota is available and eligibility is satisfied.
  10. Pay or account for the duty and import VAT due at release.
  11. Remove goods from customs control and deliver to the customer.

Along the journey — cost & liability

Every variable to price and every point at which contractual liability transfers, arranged in the order they arise on this route.

  1. Contract & pricing

    Where every cost line and legal responsibility is set.

    • Ex-mill / ex-works price. Price per tonne against the mill specification, before any handling or logistics.
    • Purchase currency & FX. Invoice currency, hedging responsibility and any non-recoverable bank charges.
    • Payment terms. Letter of credit, telegraphic transfer or open account — each has a different treasury and default profile.
    • Incoterm 2020. Sets the exact point at which cost and risk pass from seller to buyer for every subsequent stage.
    • Steel-measure allocation. Contract clause on which party bears an unexpected 50% out-of-quota duty.
    • Anti-dumping / sanctions warranty. Seller warranty on non-Russian origin and third-country-processing compliance.
    • Retention of title. Whether the seller retains title to the material until payment clears.
    • Warranty & quality. Mill certificates, testing regime and remedy for non-conformity.

  2. Arrival & entry to procedure

    Goods enter the UK but are placed under customs warehousing instead of free circulation.

    • Underlying transport cost. Whatever ocean, road, rail or air cost brought the goods to the UK gateway.
    • Warehouse declaration. CDS entry placing the consignment under the customs-warehouse procedure.
    • Warehouse-operator receipt fee. One-off fee per consignment entering the facility.
    • Sanctions & origin screening. Warehouse operator is entitled to refuse consignments in breach of restrictions.

  3. While in warehouse

    Duty and import VAT are suspended for as long as the procedure is correctly maintained.

    • Storage rate. Per pallet, coil or cubic metre per day/week; the primary running cost.
    • Handling & movement. Cost of moving stock inside the facility, restacking or sorting.
    • Insurance while in bond. Cover for the goods during warehouse storage; sometimes the warehouse operator's policy covers value up to a cap.
    • Working-capital benefit. Cash-flow value of deferred duty and import VAT until release.
    • Inventory administration. Reconciliation between warehouse stock records and commercial records.

  4. Release to free circulation

    The point at which duty, VAT and any quota claim become due.

    • Free-circulation declaration. CDS entry moving goods out of the procedure; a broker fee applies.
    • 2026 steel measure. Quota order number in CDS DE 8/1; a 50% out-of-quota tariff attaches if the quota has closed since arrival.
    • Ordinary import duty. MFN rate on the applicable commodity code where non-zero.
    • Anti-dumping & countervailing. Trade-remedy duties on top of any ordinary duty.
    • Import VAT. 20% of the customs value plus duty, usually recoverable via postponed VAT accounting.
    • Warehouse release fee. Per-consignment cost of exiting the procedure.

  5. Onward delivery

    Standard post-clearance delivery, but with the finance and audit tail attached.

    • UK inland freight. Truck or intermodal from the warehouse to the buyer.
    • Financing on realised duty and VAT. Cost of cash tied up between release and eventual sale, less the earlier suspension benefit.
    • Warehouse audit trail. Retention of entry, stock and release records for the statutory period.
    • Re-export alternative. Goods can leave the UK from bond without ever entering free circulation, avoiding duty entirely.

Key documents

  • Warehouse authorisation. HMRC customs-warehouse approval for the operator or authorised depositor.
  • Warehouse customs declaration. Filed to enter goods to the procedure on UK arrival.
  • Inventory records. Warehouse stock movements matched to underlying commercial records.
  • Free-circulation declaration. Filed at release, with quota claim in DE 8/1 where applicable.
