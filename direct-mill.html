Commercial channel · Mill contract

Direct mill purchase

Large, predictable volume purchased directly from the mill on an agreed specification. Best pricing, most control — and the most responsibility on the UK buyer.

Best suited to

Large predictable volume with established specifications and repeat cadence.

Typical transit

Determined by the chosen physical mode (sea, road, rail, air).

Cost characteristics

Potentially the lowest purchase margin, but the buyer takes on more sourcing, freight and quota risk.

Main steel risk

Minimum order sizes, quality and origin verification, and quota exposure across long lead times.

Step by step

  1. Identify and audit the actual mill.
  2. Agree exact grade, standard, dimensions, tolerances, coating and testing requirements.
  3. Obtain origin and mill traceability before negotiating the final landed price.
  4. Classify the product and model normal duty, quota availability, potential 50% duty and VAT.
  5. Agree Incoterm and payment terms.
  6. Sign the supply contract and production schedule.
  7. Book the selected freight mode.
  8. Supplier issues invoice, packing list and quality / origin documentation.
  9. Importer or broker submits the CDS entry.
  10. Claim quota if available.
  11. Clear customs and deliver.
  12. Reconcile the received material to certificates, heat numbers and customs records.

Key documents

  • Mill supply contract. Framework or spot contract with the producing mill.
  • Mill / quality certificate. Heat numbers and chemistry to support traceability.
  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial documentation.
  • Transport document. Bill of lading, CMR, air waybill or rail consignment depending on mode.
  • CDS import declaration. With quota order number in DE 8/1 where a claim applies.
