Commercial channel · Mill contract

Direct mill purchase

Large, predictable volume purchased directly from the mill on an agreed specification. Best pricing, most control — and the most responsibility on the UK buyer.

Pricing equation

Landed cost with a direct mill contract

Every cost line that lands in the buyer’s risk-adjusted per-tonne price, plus the default party who bears each. The Incoterm on the contract shifts which side pays several of these; the steel-measure and duty lines always land on the importer of record.

  1. + Mill audit & qualification cost amortised over volume buyer
  2. = Ex-mill price £ / tonne mill
  3. + MOQ premium (if small-lot) £ / tonne buyer
  4. + Pre-shipment deposit financing rate × days buyer
  5. + Mill quality inspection per lot buyer
  6. + Chosen physical route freight stack route-dependent per Incoterm
  7. + Cargo insurance % of cargo value per Incoterm
  8. + OTHC + DTHC fixed per Incoterm
  9. + Customs brokerage & CDS filing per entry buyer
  10. + UK ordinary import duty MFN % buyer
  11. + Probability × 50% steel-measure tariff quota-linked buyer
  12. + Anti-dumping & countervailing duty trade-remedy % buyer
  13. + Import VAT (recoverable via PVA) 20% buyer treasury
  14. + UK inland freight £ / tonne buyer
  15. + Storage / demurrage / detention £ / day buyer
  16. + Financing / working capital rate × days buyer
  17. + Non-recoverable indirect costs misc. buyer
Best suited to

Large predictable volume with established specifications and repeat cadence.

Typical transit

Determined by the chosen physical mode (sea, road, rail, air).

Cost characteristics

Potentially the lowest purchase margin, but the buyer takes on more sourcing, freight and quota risk.

Main steel risk

Minimum order sizes, quality and origin verification, and quota exposure across long lead times.

Step by step

  1. Identify and audit the actual mill.
  2. Agree exact grade, standard, dimensions, tolerances, coating and testing requirements.
  3. Obtain origin and mill traceability before negotiating the final landed price.
  4. Classify the product and model normal duty, quota availability, potential 50% duty and VAT.
  5. Agree Incoterm and payment terms.
  6. Sign the supply contract and production schedule.
  7. Book the selected freight mode.
  8. Supplier issues invoice, packing list and quality / origin documentation.
  9. Importer or broker submits the CDS entry.
  10. Claim quota if available.
  11. Clear customs and deliver.
  12. Reconcile the received material to certificates, heat numbers and customs records.

Along the journey — cost & liability

Every variable to price and every point at which contractual liability transfers, arranged in the order they arise on this route.

  1. Mill selection & audit

    Choosing and qualifying the mill against grade, capacity, standards and origin.

    • Audit & qualification cost. Site visit, sample testing, ISO/EN certification review.
    • Grade & standard alignment. Verification that the mill can deliver the target chemistry and mechanical properties.
    • Origin traceability. Mill certificate practice, heat-number discipline, sanctions posture.
    • Minimum-order-quantity premium. Whether the buyer’s volume meets the mill’s MOQ or attracts a small-lot uplift.

  2. Contract & pricing

    Where every cost line and legal responsibility is set.

    • Ex-mill / ex-works price. Price per tonne against the mill specification, before any handling or logistics.
    • Purchase currency & FX. Invoice currency, hedging responsibility and any non-recoverable bank charges.
    • Payment terms. Letter of credit, telegraphic transfer or open account — each has a different treasury and default profile.
    • Incoterm 2020. Sets the exact point at which cost and risk pass from seller to buyer for every subsequent stage.
    • Steel-measure allocation. Contract clause on which party bears an unexpected 50% out-of-quota duty.
    • Anti-dumping / sanctions warranty. Seller warranty on non-Russian origin and third-country-processing compliance.
    • Retention of title. Whether the seller retains title to the material until payment clears.
    • Warranty & quality. Mill certificates, testing regime and remedy for non-conformity.

  3. Production & despatch

    The lead time between order and shipment, and its cash and quality implications.

    • Production lead time. Days or weeks locked in between order confirmation and shipment.
    • Deposit & progress payments. Working-capital cost of any pre-shipment payment.
    • Quality inspection at mill. Third-party pre-shipment inspection or heat-by-heat review.
    • Rolling & packing. Any special coating, banding or export packing surcharge.
    • Mill guarantee & certificate. Chemistry and mechanical guarantee documented for each heat.

  4. Physical route & arrival

    The chosen sea, road, rail, air or waterway leg — and its own cost stack.

    • Main-carriage freight. Ocean, road, rail or air rate per the mode.
    • Cargo insurance. Whether the mill’s contract already covers this or the buyer must arrange it.
    • Origin and destination handling. OTHC and DTHC at both terminals.
    • UK arrival costs. Broker fee, ordinary duty, steel measure, sanctions and VAT as for any other route.

  5. Post-clearance & delivery

    From UK release to the buyer’s site, plus working capital and audit.

    • UK inland freight. Truck or intermodal from the UK entry point to the buyer’s site.
    • Storage, demurrage & detention. Time-based charges if the container, trailer or berth is not released promptly.
    • Financing / working capital. Cost of tying up cash between payment to the seller and sale or consumption.
    • Quality inspection at receipt. Third-party or in-house quality check on arrival.
    • Record retention. Declaration, quota confirmation, origin evidence and commercial records for the statutory audit period.

Key documents

  • Mill supply contract. Framework or spot contract with the producing mill.
  • Mill / quality certificate. Heat numbers and chemistry to support traceability.
  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial documentation.
  • Transport document. Bill of lading, CMR, air waybill or rail consignment depending on mode.
  • CDS import declaration. With quota order number in DE 8/1 where a claim applies.
