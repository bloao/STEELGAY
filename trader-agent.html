Commercial channel · Intermediary

Trader, agent or stockholder

Smaller lots and mixed grades through a trader, agent or UK stockholder. Convenient and quick — but tariff and quota risk depends on who is legally the importer.

Best suited to

Smaller lots, mixed grades and shorter lead times than a direct mill relationship supports.

Typical transit

Depends on whether goods are already in the UK or still overseas.

Cost characteristics

Trader margin, offset by less procurement complexity. UK-held stock avoids a new import process for the buyer.

Main steel risk

Identify who is legally the importer of record and who bears tariff and quota risk under the contract.

Step by step

  1. Determine whether the trader is selling goods already customs-cleared in the UK or arranging an overseas shipment specifically for you.
  2. If already cleared, the transaction is essentially a domestic purchase; the trader or importer has borne the import process.
  3. If not cleared, identify contractually who is the importer of record and who carries tariff and quota risk.
  4. Obtain mill identity, origin and specification even when buying through an intermediary.
  5. Verify tariff treatment independently.
  6. Agree price, commission and delivery terms.
  7. Trader coordinates mill and freight booking.
  8. Importer or appointed broker makes the CDS declaration.
  9. Make the quota claim where appropriate.
  10. Customs clears goods and final delivery occurs.

Along the journey — cost & liability

Every variable to price and every point at which contractual liability transfers, arranged in the order they arise on this route.

  1. Sourcing & scoping

    Finding the right stock or shipment and pinning down who really owns the risk.

    • Trader margin / commission. The intermediary’s uplift over mill or ex-warehouse price.
    • Trader credit risk. Counterparty check on solvency, sanctions and history.
    • Stocked vs. to-order. Whether the material is already in the UK or still overseas — the tariff and quota picture is very different.
    • Importer-of-record status. Whether the trader clears the goods or the buyer does.
    • Origin & mill certificate handover. Trader must pass through the underlying mill origin evidence.

  2. Contract & pricing

    Similar to a direct mill contract, but with an added layer on who bears tariff and quota risk.

    • Delivery basis (UK or overseas). Sets the point at which cost and risk pass.
    • Currency & payment terms. Often GBP for UK stock, seller currency for overseas.
    • Steel-measure allocation. Contract must state whether the trader or the buyer bears an unexpected 50% out-of-quota duty.
    • Warranty pass-through. Which mill warranties the trader assigns to the buyer.
    • Retention of title. Common in trader contracts until the invoice is paid.

  3. Physical & customs

    If the material is not already cleared, a full import journey still runs.

    • Underlying freight. Ocean, road, rail or air cost, plus insurance and terminal handling.
    • Customs brokerage & CDS filing. Filed by whichever party is importer of record.
    • Duty, steel measure & VAT. Assessed against whoever the CDS declaration names.
    • Sanctions screening. Extra important through an intermediary — the trader’s country of business is not the same as material origin.

  4. Delivery & audit

    From cleared goods to receipt, plus record-keeping if the buyer is the importer.

    • UK inland freight. To the buyer’s site (may be included by trader for UK-cleared stock).
    • Financing & working capital. Days between payment to trader and sale or use.
    • Quality inspection. Especially important with mixed-grade parcels.
    • Record retention. Import declaration, quota confirmation and origin evidence where the buyer imports.

Key documents

  • Purchase order / trader invoice. Commercial paperwork from the intermediary.
  • Origin & mill documentation. Retained for sanctions and preferential origin support.
  • Bill of lading or transport document. Where the buyer is the importer of record on the shipment.
  • CDS import declaration. Where the buyer imports; quota order number in DE 8/1 for a quota claim.
