System design

UK steel procurement intelligence system.

A nine-stage engine, a set of feeding databases, and a live supplier network. It answers, for every product on the pilot list: who’s selling it, who’s making it, what the true landed cost is now, where the market is heading, and whether to buy today or wait.

Section 2

Databases & datasets required

Fifteen data stores feed the pipeline. Some already exist (HMRC feeds); the rest need to be built or subscribed to.

A

Internal company

Own data
  • products — id, commodity code, quota category, target price, consumption, stock, safety stock, MOQ.
  • supplier_quotes — every quote received: date, product, supplier, mill, country, price, currency, Incoterm, DDP flag, freight/duty inclusion, validity, lead time, MOQ.
  • purchase_history — supplier, trader, mill, tonnes, price, dates, landed cost, quota outcome.
  • stock_history — monthly snapshots for stock, open POs, consumption, cover.
B

UK trade — HMRC

Have
  • HMRC imports 2024–2026 — commodity, origin, dispatch, transport, tonnes, value.
  • HMRC control files — commodity code → product description.
  • HMRC importer details — UK importer, address, postcode, commodity codes, active 2024/25/26, first & last seen.
C

Foreign supplier network

Build
  • market_participants — company, type (mill / trader / stockholder / producer), country, products, supplying mills, UK buyers, DDP availability, routes, evidence, active status.
  • Seeded with the Hollow Section and Merchant Bar relationships in section 3 below.
D

Foreign country export data

Public
  • France customs export statistics
  • German Destatis trade data
  • Eurostat
  • Turkish, Indian and South Korean export statistics
  • Fields: commodity, destination=UK, month, tonnes, value.
E

Shipment & supplier discovery

Third-party
  • Volza, Trademo, free bill-of-lading datasets where available.
  • Supplier websites, product catalogues, annual reports, mill certificates.
  • Distributor listings, Kallanish, supplier-network intelligence.
  • Every link tagged: Confirmed / Strongly evidenced / Probable / Possible / Unknown.
F

Quota database

Live
  • steel_quotas — category, order number, origin, quarter, initial, used, remaining, utilisation %, days remaining, exhaustion forecast.
  • Category 12B (Merchant Bar): EU 058622 · Turkey 058623 · Others 058624.
G

Transport cost database

Curated
  • transport_costs — origin, mill, mode, foreign port, UK port, depot, £/t, insurance, terminal, customs, validity.
  • EU Merchant Bar: £65–75/t depending on depot.
  • Turkish Hollow Section: sea £80–90/t · insurance £5–10/t · terminal/customs £20/t · docks → depot £20/t.
H

Route database

Supporting
  • RORO0901, PORT0203, PORT0204, PORT0205, customs declaration location data.
  • Used only to validate plausible route / port / mode. Not the primary supplier-identification method.
I

Steel price benchmarks (MEPS)

Subscribe
  • Product, region, date, benchmark £/t, monthly movement, forecast.
  • Powers the “quote vs benchmark” negotiation intelligence, e.g. quote £720/t vs MEPS £680/t = £40/t premium.
J

Raw materials

Signals
  • Scrap, iron ore, coking coal, ferroalloys where relevant.
  • Predicts mill cost pressure — falling scrap + falling energy = downward price pressure = WAIT / NEGOTIATE.
K

Energy

Signals
  • European electricity, natural gas, Turkish energy indicators, other mill-country energy costs.
  • Feeds production-cost direction.
L

Currency

Live
  • Daily GBP/EUR and GBP/USD.
  • EU offers may be EUR; Turkish mills often price against USD even where the trader invoices in GBP.
M

Shipping

Signals
  • Sea freight indices, Turkey → UK freight indications, port congestion, vessel availability, fuel/bunker movements.
  • Estimates future logistics cost.
N

Market intelligence

Curate
  • Kallanish — mill price changes, outages, cuts, new capacity, tariffs, demand, announcements.
  • Supplier network — quotes, rumours, lead-time changes, availability, sentiment.
  • NSA & industry — regulatory changes, demand developments.
  • Convert news to structured records: date · company · product · event · expected price & supply impact · confidence.
O

Demand indicators

External
  • Manufacturing: PMI, industrial production, automotive output, machinery, fabricated metals.
  • Construction: PMI, infrastructure activity, construction output, new orders.
  • Estimates whether steel consumption is likely to rise or fall.

UK Merchant Bar — producer / importer

7 Steel is a major UK producer and imports Merchant Bar sizes outside its own production range from Megasa (Spain). Some of these sizes remain inside the relevant quota category despite being unavailable from UK manufacture — worth flagging as a specific quota-competition risk in the Buying Decision engine.

Analyse the network to produce

  • Mills that supply the greatest number of traders — concentration on the supply side.
  • Traders highly dependent on one mill — supplier concentration risk.
  • Traders with diversified sourcing — safer counterparties in a squeeze.
  • Different-looking quotes that ultimately depend on the same mill.
  • Direct-mill opportunities where a trader adds no value beyond markup.
  • Where a trader adds real value: logistics, credit, aggregation, quota management, risk transfer.
  • Participants competing for the same tariff-rate quotas.
  • Alternative mills / origins if quota, price or availability deteriorates.
  • Concentration risk by product · supplier substitution options.

Section 4

DDP / import-liability analysis

Storing Incoterms against every quote and comparing DDP vs. non-DDP offers on a risk-adjusted basis. The system continues tracking the underlying import and quota even where the quoted commercial term is DDP.

Every quote records

  • Supplier / trader
  • Underlying mill where known
  • Product · origin
  • Price £/t · Incoterm
  • Transport included · duty included
  • Tariff-cap arrangement
  • Quota category
  • Lead time · MOQ · validity

Risk-adjusted comparison

DDP offer expected landed cost vs. Non-DDP supplier price + logistics + insurance + customs + expected quota/duty cost + financing = risk-adjusted landed cost

Do not assume the lowest headline supplier price is the cheapest option. Some DDP prices may include a tariff cap; buyer pays the lower net price if the tariff is avoided.

Section 5

How everything connects

Where each database feeds — internal product data drives the ask; HMRC and foreign trade data narrow the supplier universe; participant + shipment intel produces the shortlist; live market signals compose landed cost and forecast; internal stock & PO position closes the loop into the buying engine.

Feeds ↓
  1. Internal product data → What do we need?
  2. HMRC + foreign export → UK steel market picture.
  3. Supplier research + participants + shipment data → Supplier universe.
  4. → Supplier shortlist.
  5. Quotes + transport + quota + FX + CBAM → True landed cost.
  6. MEPS + scrap + energy + FX + shipping + Kallanish + network + PMI → 30 / 60 / 90-day forecast.
  7. Stock + consumption + POs + lead time + MOQ → Buying engine.
  8. → Decision.

Final objective

The one sentence the system has to be able to write.

For 50×40×5 Angle, we currently have 100 tonnes in stock and consume approximately 30 tonnes per month. Supplier A is offering £X/t DDP from Mill Y, while Supplier B is offering £Y/t non-DDP from Mill Z. Based on current quota utilisation, freight, FX, steel benchmarks, raw-material trends, market-participant behaviour and expected stock depletion, the recommended action is to purchase X tonnes from Supplier Y by DATE at a target price of £Z/t. Waiting is expected to increase / decrease procurement cost by approximately £X. Confidence: X%.
