System design

UK steel procurement intelligence system

A ten-stage engine, a set of feeding databases and a live supplier network. For every product on the pilot list it answers six questions: who’s selling it, who’s making it, what the true landed cost is on a cash-adjusted basis, where the market is heading, whether to buy today or wait, and how much of the requirement should sit with the UK domestic mill versus imports.

Section 4

DDP and import-liability analysis

Every quote is stored with its Incoterm and compared DDP vs. non-DDP on a risk-adjusted basis. The engine keeps tracking the underlying import and quota exposure even when a supplier quotes DDP — especially where the DDP price includes some or all of the potential tariff.

Every quote records

  • Supplier / trader
  • Underlying mill where known
  • Product · origin
  • Price £/t · Incoterm
  • Transport included · duty included
  • Tariff-cap arrangement
  • Quota category
  • Lead time · MOQ · validity

Risk-adjusted comparison

DDP offer expected landed cost vs. Non-DDP supplier price + logistics + insurance + customs + expected quota/duty cost + financing = risk-adjusted landed cost

Do not assume the lowest headline supplier price is the cheapest option. Some DDP prices may include a tariff cap; buyer pays the lower net price if the tariff is avoided.

Section 5 · Stage 06 in depth

Domestic vs. import sourcing and working capital

Compares Tata Steel UK against EU, Turkish and other imports on total economic procurement cost — price plus cash plus inventory plus risk plus time — not on headline £/tonne alone.

Compare on:
  • Purchase price
  • Delivered landed cost
  • Payment terms
  • Cash-flow impact
  • Inventory requirement
  • Lead time
  • Quota exposure
  • Duty & CBAM
  • FX exposure
  • Logistics risk
  • Supplier reliability
Domestic

Tata Steel UK

  • Payment terms 30 days (strict)
  • Import quota exposure None
  • CBAM exposure None
  • International freight None / substantially reduced
  • FX exposure Low or none
  • Customs / ports None
  • Lead time & MOQ Use actual company data
  • Delivery frequency Use actual company data

Main disadvantage: the strict 30-day payment term creates working-capital pressure that must be priced in.

Import

EU / Turkey / other

  • Headline price Potentially lower
  • Payment terms Often longer (60 / 90 days)
  • Quota exposure Yes — may attract the 50% out-of-quota tariff
  • CBAM Yes, on covered inputs
  • Freight, insurance, customs, terminal Yes, all layered on
  • Lead time Longer, drives higher safety stock
  • Disruption risk Ports, weather, geopolitics
  • FX exposure EUR / USD depending on origin

Main advantage: lower mill price and longer payment terms can offset the extra landed-cost lines — but only after quota, inventory and financing cost are priced in.

Total economic procurement cost

Supplier price + international transport, insurance, terminal handling, customs + UK inland transport + duty + expected quota cost + CBAM + FX impact + supplier financing cost + inventory holding cost + expected disruption cost = total economic procurement cost

Cash-flow & financing cost

funding days = customer cash-in date − supplier payment date financing cost = purchase value × annual cost of capital × funding days ÷ 365 per tonne = financing cost ÷ tonnes purchased

Illustrative: 500 t × £700 × 10% × 75/365 ≈ £7,192 ≈ £14.38/t. Tata’s economic cost becomes £714/t, not £700/t. Cost-of-capital and payment schedules come from the business, not assumptions.

Head-to-head, cash-adjusted

Option Headline £/t Payment Funding days Working-capital £/t Cash-adjusted £/t Quota & CBAM
Tata Steel UK £700 30 days 75 + £14 £714 None
EU supplier £710 landed 90 days 15 + £3 £713 Quota + CBAM apply
Turkey supplier £690 landed 60 days 45 + £8 £698 Quota exposure high

Headline £/t alone picks Tata. On cash-adjusted total economic cost the picture flips once the quota and CBAM risk on the imports is priced in on a probability-weighted basis.

Inventory requirement & holding cost

Domestic supply can justify a lower stock cover because of shorter lead times, more frequent deliveries, no customs delays and lower disruption risk.

  • Imported source — 3 months cover · 300 t · capital tied up £210,000.
  • Tata — 1.5 months cover · 150 t · capital tied up £105,000.
  • Potential working-capital release from switching to Tata: £105,000.

Holding cost per tonne includes financing, warehousing, insurance, deterioration risk, slow-moving stock and opportunity cost.

Order size & frequency

Tata’s 30-day term can also be managed by ordering smaller quantities more often. Compare:

  • 500 tonnes every 2 months
  • 125 tonnes every 2 weeks

The model optimises order quantity × frequency against payment terms, freight, MOQ, stock, lead time and available cash. Outputs: recommended order quantity, delivery frequency, peak cash requirement.

Recommend a sourcing mix, not a single supplier

The engine can split a requirement across sources. Percentages are calculated from live data, not fixed.

60% Tata Steel UK

Base requirement — short lead time, regular replenishment, lower quota & logistics risk, lower safety stock.

30% EU import

Bulk / opportunistic — lower landed cost, quota available, good supplier terms.

10% Turkey

Opportunistic — when market conditions and quota headroom favour it.

Negotiation opportunities beyond £/t

  • Longer payment terms · higher credit limit
  • Smaller MOQ · more frequent / scheduled deliveries
  • Call-off agreements · consignment stock
  • Price locks · volume rebates
  • Vendor-managed inventory

If Tata’s 30-day term itself cannot change, higher delivery frequency or smaller order sizes may reduce the working-capital impact.

External financing — is it worth it?

Tata procurement advantage before finance: £25/t Financing cost caused by payment terms: £12/t Net advantage: £13/t → Tata still attractive Tata advantage: £8/t Financing cost: £12/t Net: −£4/t → Payment terms eliminate the edge

Working-capital constraint & cash forecast

The buying engine must consider available cash — not just headline price. Inputs it must respect:

  • Available procurement cash
  • Working-capital facility
  • Supplier credit limits
  • Outstanding supplier liabilities
  • Expected customer receipts
  • Existing inventory value

Every recommended decision emits a 30 / 60 / 90-day cash impact, funding duration and estimated financing cost. A purchase is not recommended just because the £/t is attractive if it creates an unacceptable cash-flow position.

Key principle

Do not choose between Tata and imports using headline steel price alone. Compare price + cash + inventory + risk + time. The domestic option may justify a higher £/t if it reduces inventory, quota risk, CBAM, logistics, lead time and supply-chain uncertainty. Imports may still win where the landed price is sufficiently lower, quota risk is low, payment terms are better and market conditions favour them.

Section 6

How everything connects

Which inputs feed which stage, in order.

Data flow
  1. Internal product data → the ask — what do we need, how much, by when.
  2. HMRC + foreign export data → UK market picture — who imports what, from where.
  3. Supplier research + participant network + shipment data → supplier universe → shortlist.
  4. Quotes + transport + quota + FX + CBAM → true landed cost per option.
  5. Payment terms + inventory + cost of capital → cash-adjusted total economic cost.
  6. MEPS + scrap + energy + FX + shipping + Kallanish + network + PMIs → 30 / 60 / 90-day forecast.
  7. Stock + consumption + open POs + lead time + MOQ → buying engine.
  8. → Decision: supplier, mix, quantity, timing, expected saving, confidence.
