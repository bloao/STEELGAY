System design

A live intelligence layer for your existing procurement process

One interface that brings together the data your procurement process already relies on — HMRC, MEPS, supplier quotes, stock levels, consumption, FX and freight — without replacing the way you already work.

The difference is speed and visibility. Instead of waiting for monthly or quarterly reviews, key information is refreshed as close to real time as each source allows, and in real time where it matters most.

In many cases, procurement problems are not caused by a failure to act — they are caused by not knowing, in time, that action was needed.

The first objective is therefore to remove that information lag, so decisions can be made while they still have an impact. Once that foundation is fast, reliable and trusted, a forecasting layer can be added on top to provide a forward-looking view of risk, cost and opportunity.

Final objective

The one sentence the system has to be able to write

This is what the whole engine is built to produce for every product on the pilot list. Everything that follows — the ten stages, the fifteen data sources, the supplier network, the working-capital module — exists to make this sentence answerable with evidence.

For 50×40×5 Angle, we currently have 100 tonnes in stock and consume approximately 30 tonnes per month. Supplier A is offering £X/t DDP from Mill Y, while Supplier B is offering £Y/t non-DDP from Mill Z. Based on current quota utilisation, freight, FX, steel benchmarks, raw-material trends, market-participant behaviour and expected stock depletion, the recommended action is to purchase X tonnes from Supplier Y by DATE at a target price of £Z/t. Waiting is expected to increase / decrease procurement cost by approximately £X. Confidence: X%.

Reading guide

Section 1 and Section 2, and how they fit together

Section 1 defines the calculations. Section 2 stores the numbers those calculations need. Every stage in Section 1 reaches down into specific fields in the Section 2 data sources — that’s the chain shown here.

Section 1 — the foundation

The foundation of the whole system is agreeing the core framework. For each product, we need to align on the stages of the supply chain, the assumptions behind each calculation, the variables that matter, and the decision points that affect cost, risk and margin. This includes freight, duty, quota exposure, FX, expected demand, payment terms and supplier reliability. Once these inputs are agreed, the Section 2 numbers can be applied consistently across the model.

How they connect — the chain

Each calculation depends on a clear link between the variable, the data point that supplies it, and the field in the Section 2 schema it is read from.

The model does not invent numbers. It pulls agreed values from agreed locations and uses them consistently across each stage.

Section 2 — the data that feeds it

Section 2 defines the data layer behind the model.

For each source, we need to align on the data points it contains, where they sit, who owns them, how often they update, and what the schema looks like.

Once this is agreed, the model can read the right inputs from the right places in the right format.

Section 1 · The engine

Overall flow

Ten stages, always left to right — top row 01–05, then bottom row 06–10. Each stage is a supply-chain equation (see the chain above).

  • ShapeS-flow, 5 × 2 grid
  • Each cellInputs above, output below

  • Final stage10 — per-product recommendation
  1. 01

    Our product & demand baseline

    Fixed

    Our fixed truth — what we sell, what we hold, what we’ll pay. The constraint every downstream stage is built to satisfy.

    • Our product list · dimensions · commodity code · quota category
    • Our demand: monthly consumption · current stock · projected stock-out
    • Our operational constraints: MOQ we accept · cover we hold · safety stock we protect
    • Our commercial ceiling: target £/t buy price we won’t exceed
  2. 02

    UK market mapping

    HMRC

    The addressable market for this commodity code.

    • Rolling 12-month UK import volume for this commodity code
    • Origin & dispatch countries with market share and trend
    • Active UK importers — competitors and neutrals
    • Transport modes and typical UK entry ports
  3. 03

    Approved supplier universe

    Restricted

    The suppliers we can actually depend on for this product — approved, qualified, ours.

    • Approved supplier list for this product (mill · trader · stockholder)
    • Status per supplier: active · qualified · preferred · restricted
    • What each can supply: dimensions · grade · DDP availability · MOQ
    • Fallback set — approved alternates if the primary is unavailable
  4. 04

    Market participant analysis

    Network

    Assesses the candidates and picks the shortlist.

    • Independent-mill count behind the shortlist — real concentration risk
    • Shared-mill exposure — which traders quote the same source
    • Peer / competitor buying patterns from these suppliers
    • Shortlist of realistic suppliers to invite to quote
  5. 05

    True landed cost engine

    Live

    Converts every candidate offer to £/t delivered, risk-adjusted.

    • Supplier price + Incoterm normalisation
    • + freight · insurance · terminal · customs · inland
    • + duty · quota uplift (P(exhausted)×50%) · CBAM
    • + FX adjustment · financing cost of payment terms
    • = risk-adjusted £/t delivered, per candidate
  6. 06

    Risk envelope & time exposure

    Uncertainty

    First Part 2 stage — takes the fixed Stage 05 numbers and asks what could shift between now and shipment.

    • Which Stage 05 variables carry time exposure (quota, FX, freight, MEPS)
    • Payment-terms exposure per candidate (working-capital days tied up)
    • Cash at risk per option — financing cost of the WC gap, £/t
    • Range of outcomes per unknown — best case · base · stress
    • Frames the risk envelope that stages 07–08 populate with forecasts
  7. 07

    Market forecast engine

    Signals

    Turns market signals into directional pressure on the definitive variables.

    • MEPS + scrap + iron ore + coking coal → mill cost pressure
    • Energy + FX + freight indices → landed-cost drift
    • Quota burn rate + market intelligence → supply constraint signal
    • Manufacturing + construction PMIs → demand direction
  8. 08

    30 / 60 / 90-day forecast

    Forecast

    Probabilistic forward view for our specific product.

    • Expected replacement £/t at 30 · 60 · 90 days
    • P(price ↑) vs P(price ↓) with confidence band
    • P(quota exhausts before our shipment date)
    • Supplier availability & lead-time risk
  9. 09

    Buying decision

    Action

    Selects the action that maximises expected margin. All upstream stages converge here.

    BUY NOW WAIT NEGOTIATE SWITCH SUPPLIER SWITCH COUNTRY BUY MORE / LESS ACCELERATE
  10. 10

    Final output

    Deliverable

    The exemplar sentence, populated for one product.

    • Recommended supplier & underlying mill
    • Recommended origin & sourcing mix (if split)
    • Target £/t · expected landed £/t
    • Recommended quantity & required delivery date
    • Peak cash requirement · funding days
    • Expected saving vs baseline · confidence %

Section 2 ↔ populates the Section 1 engine

Data sources and datasets required

Fifteen data sources feed the pipeline. HMRC feeds already exist; the rest need to be built or subscribed to. Each card lists purpose, the datasets it holds, and how it’s used. Every field named here is a candidate data point in the stage → equation → variable → data point → data source chain.

How the fifteen fit together

  1. A tells us what we need.
  2. B + D map the UK market and flows.
  3. C + E + H identify who can supply it.
  4. F + G + L + A5 give the true landed cost.
  5. I + J + K + L + M + N + O forecast the market 30/60/90 days out.
  6. All of the above → the procurement engine picks a decision.
BUY NOW WAIT NEGOTIATE SWITCH SUPPLIER SWITCH ORIGIN DOMESTIC + IMPORT MIX ACCELERATE DELIVERY

The goal isn’t the cheapest quote — it’s the lowest risk-adjusted, cash-adjusted total procurement cost while keeping stock intact.

What the tool produces — sourcing analysis, in the UI

Once the engine runs, this is what appears in the tool: ranked sourcing options compared on total economic procurement cost, not headline £/tonne alone. Numbers below are illustrative — the real ones are calculated from live business data.

Domestic

Tata Steel UK

Strengths: no quota, no CBAM, minimal FX, short lead time, low disruption risk.

Trade-off: 30-day payment term creates working-capital pressure that must be priced in.

Import

EU / Turkey / other

Strengths: often lower mill price, longer payment terms (60–90 days), more sourcing options.

Trade-off: quota, CBAM, FX, freight and longer lead times all need to be priced in before comparing.

Total economic procurement cost

Price + landed-cost lines + cash, risk & time costs = total economic £/tonne

The cheapest headline £/tonne does not always win. Once cash, risk and time are included, the ranking often flips.

Putting it all together

How it works, end to end

Data flows in from many sources. Each supply chain resolves its own equation into one risk-adjusted price. Those prices meet our stock and cash constraints, the aggregation engine picks the best mix, and the recommendation drops out at the bottom.
