Physical route · Roll-on/roll-off & road haulage

Road / RoRo freight

European road and roll-on/roll-off flows for EU-origin coil, plate, bar and fabricated steel where lead time and flexibility matter more than absolute unit cost.

Pricing equation

Risk-adjusted landed cost per tonne (road / RoRo)

Every cost line that lands in the buyer’s risk-adjusted per-tonne price, plus the default party who bears each. The Incoterm on the contract shifts which side pays several of these; the steel-measure and duty lines always land on the importer of record.

  1. = Ex-mill or ex-works price £ / tonne seller
  2. + Origin inland freight, mill to loading point £ / tonne per Incoterm
  3. + Full-truckload rate € / km per Incoterm
  4. + Fuel & toll surcharges % or per km carrier
  5. + Ferry or Eurotunnel booking per trailer carrier / buyer
  6. + CMR carrier liability + top-up cargo insurance % of cargo value buyer typically
  7. + GVMS / GMR admin per movement buyer / broker
  8. + Customs brokerage & CDS filing per entry buyer
  9. + UK ordinary import duty MFN % buyer
  10. + Probability × 50% steel-measure tariff quota-linked buyer
  11. + Anti-dumping & countervailing duty trade-remedy % buyer
  12. + Import VAT (recoverable via PVA) 20% buyer treasury
  13. + UK inland freight £ / tonne buyer
  14. + Storage / demurrage / detention £ / day buyer
  15. + Financing / working capital rate × days buyer
  16. + Non-recoverable indirect costs misc. buyer
Best suited to

EU-origin coils, plate, bars, fabricated steel and just-in-time loads.

Typical transit

Commonly 1–3 days from nearby continental markets before disruption and clearance.

Cost characteristics

Higher £ per tonne than large sea lots; 2025/26 European FTL benchmarks roughly €1.10–€1.90 per km.

Main steel risk

Very short lead times mean the live quota balance must be checked immediately before the CDS claim.

Step by step

  1. Qualify supplier and origin exactly as for sea freight.
  2. Classify the steel and check tariff, quota, sanctions and trade remedies.
  3. Agree Incoterm and book a compliant heavy-goods vehicle or trailer.
  4. Prepare invoice, packing list, origin evidence, mill certificate as necessary and CMR road consignment note.
  5. Prepare or pre-lodge the CDS import declaration and obtain the relevant customs movement reference.
  6. Where the route uses the Goods Vehicle Movement Service, link the required declarations into the Goods Movement Reference.
  7. Carrier completes required Entry Summary Declaration / safety-security requirements.
  8. Vehicle checks in for ferry or Eurotunnel movement.
  9. On arrival, comply with any inspection or customs instruction.
  10. Quota is allocated if valid and available; otherwise the applicable out-of-quota tariff applies.
  11. Account for duty and VAT and release the vehicle for UK delivery.

Along the journey — cost & liability

Every variable to price and every point at which contractual liability transfers, arranged in the order they arise on this route.

  1. Contract & pricing

    Where every cost line and legal responsibility is set.

    • Ex-mill / ex-works price. Price per tonne against the mill specification, before any handling or logistics.
    • Purchase currency & FX. Invoice currency, hedging responsibility and any non-recoverable bank charges.
    • Payment terms. Letter of credit, telegraphic transfer or open account — each has a different treasury and default profile.
    • Incoterm 2020. Sets the exact point at which cost and risk pass from seller to buyer for every subsequent stage.
    • Steel-measure allocation. Contract clause on which party bears an unexpected 50% out-of-quota duty.
    • Anti-dumping / sanctions warranty. Seller warranty on non-Russian origin and third-country-processing compliance.
    • Retention of title. Whether the seller retains title to the material until payment clears.
    • Warranty & quality. Mill certificates, testing regime and remedy for non-conformity.

  2. At origin

    From the mill to the outbound port, terminal or airport.

    • Inland origin freight. Truck, rail or barge cost from the mill to the export point.
    • Export documentation. Commercial invoice, packing list, mill certificate, origin declaration.
    • Export customs formalities. Usually the seller’s obligation; the cost varies by jurisdiction.
    • Origin terminal handling (OTHC). Loading, stuffing and outbound port or terminal charges.
    • Insurance up to load point. Cover for the inland leg to the export gateway.

  3. Main transport

    The road / RoRo leg — typically 1–3 days from the EU near-continent.

    • Full-truckload rate. European FTL benchmarks roughly €1.10–€1.90 per km for 2025/26.
    • Fuel & toll surcharges. Diesel and road-toll levies on top of the base rate.
    • Ferry or Eurotunnel booking. RoRo capacity fee, plus any hazardous- or overweight-load supplement.
    • Cargo insurance in transit. Truck operator liability under CMR is limited; top-up cargo insurance is common.
    • GVMS / GMR paperwork. Goods Movement Reference linkage for eligible RoRo ports.
    • Driver & equipment demurrage. Hourly charge if loading, clearance or delivery windows overrun.

  4. UK arrival & customs

    Duty, VAT and every trade-policy check applied to the consignment.

    • Destination terminal handling (DTHC). Discharge and outbound handling at the UK port, terminal or airport.
    • Customs brokerage & CDS filing. Broker fee or in-house filing cost for the import declaration.
    • UK ordinary import duty. MFN rate on the applicable commodity code, where any survives preferential treatment.
    • 2026 steel measure. Quota order number claim in CDS DE 8/1; a 50% out-of-quota tariff attaches if the quota has closed.
    • Anti-dumping & countervailing. Separate trade-remedy duties on top of ordinary duty and the steel measure.
    • Sanctions screening. Russian-origin iron and steel and third-country-processed material prohibitions.
    • Import VAT. 20% of customs value plus duty, usually recoverable via postponed VAT accounting.

  5. Post-clearance & delivery

    From UK release to the buyer’s site, plus working capital and audit.

    • UK inland freight. Truck or intermodal from the UK entry point to the buyer’s site.
    • Storage, demurrage & detention. Time-based charges if the container, trailer or berth is not released promptly.
    • Financing / working capital. Cost of tying up cash between payment to the seller and sale or consumption.
    • Quality inspection at receipt. Third-party or in-house quality check on arrival.
    • Record retention. Declaration, quota confirmation, origin evidence and commercial records for the statutory audit period.

Key documents

  • Commercial invoice & packing list. Standard commercial paperwork for the load.
  • CMR consignment note. International road-freight transport document.
  • GMR / MRN. Goods Movement Reference and CDS master reference for RoRo ports on GVMS.
  • Origin evidence. Preferential proof of origin where a reduced-tariff claim is made.
  • CDS import declaration. Six-digit quota order number in DE 8/1 for a quota claim.
